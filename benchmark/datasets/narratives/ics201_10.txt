**Section 4 — Map/Sketch**

The total area of operations spans an 8-mile-by-4-mile polygon bounded to the north by Ridgeline Road, to the east by the Klamath River corridor, to the south by Pinecrest Drive residential zone, and to the west by the Redwood Bluff Substation access road — approximately 32 square miles of operational footprint.

The incident site is the ignition zone at PG&E transmission Tower 14-B near the Redwood Bluff substation, a 0.4-acre initial footprint expanding northeast.

Impacted areas include the full chaparral fuel bed along the southwest slope of Ridgeline, 12 structures on the north end of Pinecrest Drive confirmed ignited and destroyed, and 2.1 miles of Ridgeline Road impassable due to active flame fronts and debris.

Threatened areas: Pinecrest Drive residential neighborhood (87 parcels, approximately 210 residents under mandatory evacuation order issued at 23:15 March 14); the Redwood Bluff Volunteer Fire Station on Skyline Avenue; and the Klamath River watershed and riparian habitat beginning 1.4 miles northeast of the active perimeter.

Overflight results: Helicopter recon at 04:15 March 15 confirmed a continuous running crown fire along the northeast ridge with ember cast ahead of the main front reaching 0.6 miles, and a slow-moving ground fire in a meadow pocket on the south flank.

Trajectory: Primary spread vector is ENE at approximately 2.5 mph under current conditions; a forecast wind shift to southwesterly by 14:00 March 15 could redirect the head fire directly toward the Klamath River campground cluster.

Impacted shorelines: Approximately 0.8 miles of Klamath River riparian buffer have experienced surface ember deposition; no water quality impacts confirmed.

Graphics and symbology: The tactical map is oriented with North at top, uses NWCG standard color symbology (red for perimeter, green for containment lines, blue for water sources), and has been geo-referenced and uploaded to the ICS 204 resource tracking tablets.

**Section 5 — Situation Summary and Health/Safety Briefing**

Situation Summary: The Redwood Bluff WUI Fire ignited from electrical arcing at the Redwood Bluff substation during red flag conditions (9% RH, 18 mph offshore winds gusting to 34 mph, fine dead fuel moisture at 4%) on the evening of March 14, 2027. Rapid spotting across Ridgeline Road overwhelmed initial attack resources, leading to a Type 3 extended attack declaration at 00:30 March 15. By the morning briefing, 340 acres have burned, 12 structures are confirmed destroyed, 87 parcels remain under mandatory evacuation, and containment is at 15% on the southern and western flanks. The primary risk for this operational period is the forecast afternoon wind shift which could transition the fire from a northeast run to a direct threat toward the Klamath River recreation corridor.

Health and Safety Hazards: Extreme fire behavior risk from predicted afternoon wind shift; snag and falling tree hazard in burn areas (hard hats mandatory, chainsaw crews maintain 2-tree-length spacing); aviation conflict risk near designated drop zone corridors (no ground personnel within 500 feet without coordination with Air Attack Supervisor); heat and hydration stress (forecast 91°F, mandatory hydration check-ins every 30 minutes); smoke inhalation hazard.

Necessary Measures: All personnel must identify and communicate their escape route and safety zone to their supervisor before 13:00. Sign-in on accountability board at each division required. Radio communication on Tactical Channel 3 (simplex) mandatory. LCES (Lookouts, Communications, Escape Routes, Safety Zones) briefed and confirmed at every morning assignment. N95 minimum for all personnel outside containment lines; SCBA required for any entry into active burn areas. Dozer operators must have a foot crew escort when working within 200 feet of the active fire edge.
