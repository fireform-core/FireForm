**PART III — CURRENT SITUATION AND MAP OVERVIEW (Written as an after-action prose memo, not a structured briefing)**

Crews have been fighting this fire since late in the evening. What started as a power line arc ignition near the Redwood Bluff substation around nine forty-five PM on March 14, 2027 — formally captured in dispatch logs as 21:45 PST, incident number CWPP-KNF-2027-0314-RB — quickly established itself in a dense chaparral-oak interface and spotted aggressively across Ridgeline Road within the first ninety minutes. At the time of this writing (morning briefing, March 15 at 07:30), the fire has burned approximately 340 acres and is 15% contained.

The total area of operations spans a roughly 8-mile-by-4-mile polygon bounded to the north by Ridgeline Road, to the east by the Klamath River corridor, to the south by Pinecrest Drive residential zone, and to the west by the Redwood Bluff Substation access road — approximately 32 square miles of operational footprint. The incident site proper is the ignition zone at the base of the substation transmission tower (Tower 14-B on PG&E infrastructure maps), a 0.4-acre initial footprint that has expanded northeast under the influence of offshore drainage winds averaging 18 mph with gusts to 34 mph. Impacted areas include the full chaparral fuel bed along the southwest slope of Ridgeline, 12 structures on the north end of Pinecrest Drive confirmed ignited and destroyed, and 2.1 miles of Ridgeline Road rendered impassable due to active flame fronts and debris. Threatened areas of highest priority are: the Pinecrest Drive residential neighborhood (87 parcels, approximately 210 residents under mandatory evacuation order issued at 23:15 March 14), the Redwood Bluff Volunteer Fire Station on Skyline Avenue (currently being used as an equipment cache and must be protected), and the Klamath River watershed and associated riparian habitat beginning 1.4 miles northeast of the active perimeter. Overflight results from the initial helicopter recon at 04:15 March 15 confirmed a continuous running crown fire along the northeast ridge with ember cast ahead of the main front reaching 0.6 miles, and a slow-moving ground fire in a meadow pocket on the south flank that poses minimal immediate threat but must be monitored for wind-shift risk. Trajectory modeling from the Fire Behavior Analyst assigned at shift start indicates the primary spread vector is ENE at approximately 2.5 mph under current conditions, but a forecast wind shift to southwesterly by 14:00 March 15 could redirect the head fire directly toward the Klamath River campground cluster. Impacted shorelines along the Klamath River: approximately 0.8 miles of riparian buffer have experienced surface ember deposition; no water quality impacts confirmed yet. Map graphics and symbology: the tactical map is oriented with North at top, uses NWCG standard color symbology (red for perimeter, green for containment lines, blue for water sources), and has been geo-referenced and uploaded to the ICS 204 resource tracking tablets.

**PART IV — HEALTH, SAFETY, AND SITUATION SUMMARY (Reversed order: hazards before summary, as requested by Safety Officer)**

The Safety Officer has flagged the following hazards as life-safety priorities: (1) extreme fire behavior risk from predicted afternoon wind shift — all personnel must identify and communicate their escape route and safety zone to their supervisor before 13:00; (2) snag and falling tree hazard throughout Division Alpha and Division Bravo burn area — hard hats mandatory, chainsaw crews must maintain 2-tree-length spacing protocol; (3) aviation conflict risk — no ground personnel shall operate within 500 feet of any designated drop zone corridor without direct coordination with Air Attack Supervisor Hawk Suárez; (4) heat and hydration stress — ambient temperature forecast to reach 91°F by early afternoon, mandatory hydration check-ins every 30 minutes, ICS 206 protocol in effect; (5) smoke inhalation — N95 minimum for all unprotected personnel outside the containment lines, SCBA for any entry into active burn areas. Necessary protective measures in force: all personnel must sign into the accountability board at each division before beginning work, two-way radio communication on Tactical Channel 3 (simplex) is mandatory for all field crews, LCES (Lookouts, Communications, Escape Routes, Safety Zones) must be briefed and confirmed at every morning assignment, and all dozer operators must have a foot crew escort when working within 200 feet of the active fire edge.

Now for the situation summary proper: the Redwood Bluff Wildland-Urban Interface Fire ignited from electrical arcing at the Redwood Bluff substation during red flag conditions (low relative humidity of 9%, offshore winds 18 mph gusting to 34 mph, fine dead fuel moisture at 4%) on the evening of March 14, 2027. Rapid spotting across Ridgeline Road in the first hour overwhelmed initial attack resources, leading to a transition to extended attack and a declaration of a Type 3 incident at 00:30 March 15. By the morning briefing, 340 acres have burned, 12 structures are confirmed destroyed, 87 parcels remain under mandatory evacuation, and containment is at 15% on the southern and western flanks. The primary risk for the operational period is the forecast afternoon wind shift which could transition the fire from a northeast run to a direct threat toward the Klamath River recreation corridor.
