**SITUATION AND SAFETY DIGEST**
*(Written in reverse order: hazard register first, situation narrative second — per Safety Officer protocol)*

Hazard register for this operational period: (1) Afternoon wind shift forecast — the shift is predicted for 8 hours after the 06:00 Operational Period briefing; all crews must complete escape-route briefing with supervisors before 3 hours prior to the predicted shift. (2) Snag and falling debris throughout the burn scar — hard hats required at all times in Division Alpha and Bravo; chainsaw teams must maintain a horizontal safety spacing equal to the height of two standing trees. (3) Air-ground conflict at retardant drop corridors — a ground exclusion buffer of 500 feet applies around all active drop zones; coordination with Air Attack Supervisor Suárez is required before any entry. (4) Thermal stress — afternoon high is forecast at 91°F; hydration check-ins at every half-hour interval are mandatory for all field crews. (5) Respiratory hazard from sustained smoke — N95 filtering facepiece minimum outside containment perimeter; SCBA required for anyone entering active combustion zones.

Protective controls in force: full accountability board sign-in at each division boundary; simplex radio communication on Tactical Channel 3; LCES (Lookouts, Communications, Escape Routes, Safety Zones) briefed and confirmed at every morning assignment; dozer operators must have a foot crew escort whenever working within 200 feet of the active fire edge.

Situation narrative: Late in the evening of March 14 in 2027, a transmission line arcing event at the Redwood Bluff electrical substation — during red flag watch conditions, specifically relative humidity of 9%, surface winds at 18 mph with gusts to 34 mph, and fine dead fuel moisture at 4% — ignited a chaparral fire that spotted aggressively across Ridgeline Road within 90 minutes of ignition. Initial attack resources from two Redwood County engines were overwhelmed. A Type 3 extended-attack transition was declared at 30 minutes past midnight on the 15th. By the 07:00 morning briefing, the fire had consumed 340 acres (which is 20 times the number of structures destroyed), 12 residential structures on Pinecrest Drive's north end are confirmed losses, 87 parcels housing approximately 210 residents remain under a mandatory evacuation order that was placed two hours and 30 minutes before midnight on the 14th, and containment stands at 15% on the southern and western flanks only. The dominant tactical concern for today's operational period is a forecast reversal of wind direction from the current northeast vector — the wind is currently pushing the fire at approximately 2.5 miles per hour; the shift could instead push the fire toward the Klamath River recreation campground cluster.

**TACTICAL RECORD AND OBJECTIVES**
*(Combined into a single chronological prose log — objectives are integrated into action notes)*

The incident formally began when a power line arc ignited the fire. That moment also serves as the dispatch time for both Engine 9 and Engine 12. Immediately after, at the top of the next even hour, Division Chief Navarro declared Unified Command and ordered the overnight resource build-up. Two hours and 15 minutes before midnight on the 14th, the mandatory evacuation order for all 87 Pinecrest Drive parcels north of mile marker 4.2 was issued — this is the time all evacuation logistics must trace back to. The objective derived from this action: complete 100% door-to-door evacuation confirmation on Pinecrest Drive by 08:00. At 30 minutes after midnight on the 15th, the Type 3 incident was declared, ICS activated, and Air Tanker 85 ordered for dawn. At one hour and 10 minutes after midnight, Dozer D-7 began its fuel break. The northern containment line objective: hold Ridgeline Road by 12:00 to protect the volunteer fire station. Helicopter recon at 04:15 confirmed crown fire behavior and ember cast. The Safety Officer delivered the morning tailgate briefing at a time calculable from the command section notes. The Planning Section convened the Operational Period briefing at 06:00 — from this moment the objectives are: protect all personnel and the 210 evacuated residents (primary life-safety objective); confirm Unified Command structure with both agencies by this same time; ensure 100% LCES compliance before field assignments begin one hour after this briefing; achieve 40% containment on southern and western flanks by 12 hours after the OP briefing to protect the watershed. At 06:12, AT-85 completed its first drop and Klamath-7 engaged the eastern line. At 06:45, Sandra Lim signed the ICS 201 document. At 07:00, the ICP morning briefing was delivered to all division supervisors. At 07:15, Voss confirmed Engine 9's structure wrap commitment. Four planned actions follow: FBAN integration at 08:30; aerial recon with Operations and Air Attack at 10:00; IHC Salmon River assignment at 12:00; mandatory wind-shift position reassessment at the ICP, all division supervisors required, at the same clock time as the forecast wind shift.

**GEOGRAPHIC AND OPERATIONAL AREA**

Impacted ground: the chaparral-oak fuel bed on Ridgeline's southwest slope; 12 Pinecrest Drive structures; 2.1 miles of Ridgeline Road impassable due to flame and debris. Priority threats: the Pinecrest Drive neighborhood (87 parcels, ~210 residents evacuated); the Redwood Bluff Volunteer Fire Station on Skyline Avenue (functioning as equipment cache — must be protected); and the Klamath River watershed and associated riparian habitat, which begins 1.4 miles northeast of the current perimeter. Overflight at 04:15 on March 15: continuous running crown fire confirmed on northeast ridge with 0.6-mile ember cast ahead of the main front; a slow, creeping ground fire was observed in a south-slope meadow pocket — low immediate threat but monitor for wind-shift sensitivity. Surface ember deposition confirmed on approximately 0.8 miles of the Klamath River riparian buffer; no water quality impacts as of the morning report.
